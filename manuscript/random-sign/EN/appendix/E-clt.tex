\section{The Gaussian limit after failure and the Gaussian rule}\label{app:clt}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:clt} and Corollary~\ref{cor:gauss}. Write $D_v$ for the degree of cell $v$ and $l_i=\sum_{j\ne i}H_{ij}$. We first prove a Gaussian limit for the quadratic form in the random signs on a deterministic good graph, uniformly over good graphs (Lemma~\ref{lem:cumulant}). We then show that good graphs retain almost all probability after conditioning on failure. For the $d=o(M)$ and overload regimes, we use the Gaussian limit under deterministic coefficients (Lemma~\ref{lem:plantclt}). This limit is uniform in the coefficients and holds under both the random mapping and the planted distribution. Finally, we consider the possible limits of $d/M$ and combine the cases through a subsequence argument.

\subsection{CLT for the random signs on a good graph}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cumulant}
Fix $k\ge3$. Let a deterministic sequence of graphs satisfy $\Omega_2/M\to\beta\in(0,\infty)$ and $\max_il_i=O(\log M)$. Then $\Omega_2^{-1/2}\sum_{i<j}H_{ij}\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j\Rightarrow N(0,1)$, uniformly over graphs satisfying the same constant bounds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
There are at most $\Omega_2=O(M)$ nonzero summands, each bounded by $k$. Two summands can be dependent only if they share a key, so the dependency graph has maximum degree at most $2\max_il_i=O(\log M)$. Distinct summands have zero covariance (Appendix~\ref{app:encoding}), so the sum has variance exactly $\Omega_2$. For any fixed order $r\ge3$, a joint cumulant can be nonzero only if the summands form a connected group in the dependency graph \cite{janson1988cumulant}. There are $O_r(M(\log M)^{r-1})$ connected groups, each with a bounded cumulant. Dividing by $\Omega_2^{r/2}\asymp M^{r/2}$ gives $O_r(M^{1-r/2}(\log M)^{r-1})\to0$. The first cumulant is zero and the second is one. The standard Gaussian is determined by its moments, so the method of moments gives convergence. These bounds depend only on the range of $\beta$ and the constant in $\max_il_i$. They are therefore uniform over graphs satisfying the same constant bounds. If CDF convergence were not uniform, we could extract a sequence of deterministic good graphs along which convergence fails, contradicting the argument above.

The difference between $\widehat d-d$ and $\frac{2}{k(1-k/M)}$ times this sum is
\begin{equation*}
-\frac{k}{M(1-k/M)}\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_i\varepsilon_i\Bigr)^2-d\Bigr],
\end{equation*}
which depends only on the signs and has $L^2$ norm $O(d/M)$ for every graph. After division by $s(d)\asymp d/\sqrt M$, this norm is $O(M^{-1/2})$. On a good graph from Lemma~\ref{lem:conc}, $\Omega_2$ equals its exact mean plus $O(\sqrt M(\log M)^4)$. Thus the ratio of $\frac{4\Omega_2}{k^2(1-k/M)^2}$ to $s(d)^2$ tends to one, and we can replace normalization by $\sqrt{\Omega_2}$ in this lemma with normalization by $s(d)$. The standardized statistic $X$ for $\widehat d$ therefore has the same uniform Gaussian limit on good graphs. By \eqref{eq:raw-decomp}, the difference between $\widetilde d$ and $\widehat d$ is $\frac kM[(\sum_i\varepsilon_i)^2-\widehat d]$. Given the graph, $(\sum_i\varepsilon_i)^2-d$ has $L^2$ norm $\sqrt{2d(d-1)}$, and $\widehat d$ is conditionally unbiased with conditional variance at most $(M-1)s(d)^2=2d(d-1)$. By the triangle inequality, the difference has conditional $L^2$ norm at most $\frac{2k}M\sqrt{2d(d-1)}$. After division by $s(d)$, this is at most $2k\sqrt{M-1}/M\to0$. Hence the same Gaussian limit holds for $\widetilde d$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditioning on failure at a positive load ratio}
For $d\ge2$, a specified duplicate edge pair forms a core, so
\begin{equation*}
\Pr(F)\ge\Pr(S_1=S_2)=\binom Mk^{-1}.
\end{equation*}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:conc}, $\Pr(\mathcal G\notin\mathcal G_M\mid F)\le\Pr(\mathcal G\notin\mathcal G_M)\binom Mk\to0$. The conditional CDF error is at most the uniform error on good graphs plus this conditional exceptional probability. Thus, at a positive load ratio,
\begin{equation*}
\sup_x\bigl|\Pr(X\le x\mid F)-\Phi(x)\bigr|\to0.
\end{equation*}
The same argument applies to any event that depends only on the mapping and has probability at least a fixed negative power of $M$. We divide the superpolynomially small exceptional probability by $\Pr(F)$ and average the CLT error on good graphs under the conditional distribution. The good-graph CLT therefore requires only convergence to zero, regardless of how rare failure is. A typical floor failure arises from a duplicate edge pair, which sets just one $H_{ij}$ to $k$. The resulting change in $\Omega_2$ is $O(1)$, far below the deviation allowed by Lemma~\ref{lem:conc}, so the graph remains in $\mathcal G_M$.

The conditional variance is also asymptotic to $s(d)^2$. On good graphs, the ratio tends to one by \eqref{eq:condmoments} and Lemma~\ref{lem:conc}. On any graph, the ratio is at most $M-1$, and the superpolynomially small probability of bad graphs controls their contribution.

\subsection{Gaussian limit under deterministic coefficients}
The following Gaussian limit holds uniformly over deterministic coefficient vectors $(s_i)\in\{\pm1\}^d$. We apply it to random sign encoding by conditioning on the iid coding coefficients and then averaging over the signs.

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian limit under deterministic coefficients]\label{lem:plantclt}
For deterministic coefficients $(s_i)\in\{\pm1\}^d$, let $C_v=\sum_{i:v\in S_i}s_i$, compute $\widehat d$ by \eqref{eq:dhat}, and define $X$ by \eqref{eq:X}. If $d/\sqrt M\to\infty$, then $X\Rightarrow N(0,1)$, uniformly in the coefficients, with $d/M$ allowed to be unbounded. Under the planted distribution $Q$, we also have $X\Rightarrow N(0,1)$, uniformly in the coefficients and the label pair.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $Y=\widehat d-d$, $W_{ij}=H_{ij}-k^2/M$, and $v_i=\mathbf1_{S_i}-(k/M)\mathbf1$. We expose edges in label order. The martingale differences are $\Delta_j=\frac{2}{k(1-k/M)}s_jv_j^\top\sum_{i<j}s_iv_i$, with $Y=\sum_j\Delta_j$. Their conditional variances sum to
\begin{gather*}
\mathcal V_d=s(d)^2+\frac{8}{k(1-k/M)(M-1)}\sum_{i<\ell}(d-\ell)s_is_\ell W_{i\ell},\\
\Var\mathcal V_d=\frac{64}{(M-1)^3}\sum_{\ell=2}^d(\ell-1)(d-\ell)^2=O\Bigl(\frac{d^4}{M^3}\Bigr).
\end{gather*}
Since $s(d)^4\approx4d^4/M^2$, the normalized bracket $\mathcal V_d/s(d)^2$ has variance $O(1/M)$ and converges to one in $L^2$. Given the first $j-1$ edges, the components of $\sum_{i<j}s_iv_i$ sum to zero. Thus $v_j^\top\sum_{i<j}s_iv_i$ is the sum of this vector's components over the $k$ cells of $S_j$. Its conditional fourth moment is at most $k^3\cdot\frac kM$ times the sum of the fourth powers of all components. The component at cell $v$, $\sum_{i<j}s_i(\mathbf1\{v\in S_i\}-k/M)$, is a sum of $j-1$ independent, centered, bounded terms, each with variance at most $k/M$. Rosenthal's inequality \cite{rosenthal1970} bounds its fourth moment by $C_k(j/M+j^2/M^2)$. Hence $\E\Delta_j^4\le C_k(j/M+j^2/M^2)$. Summing over $j\le d$ and dividing by $s(d)^4$, we bound the fourth-order Lyapunov remainder by
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\sum_j\E\Delta_j^4}{s(d)^4}\le C_k\Bigl(\frac M{d^2}+\frac1d\Bigr)\to0,
\end{equation*}
and the martingale CLT \cite{hall1980martingale} gives $X\Rightarrow N(0,1)$. Both bounds depend only on $|s_i|=1$, so convergence is uniform in the coefficients and holds for unbounded $d/M$.

Under the planted distribution, the statistic is invariant under label permutation. We may therefore fix the duplicate edge pair as 1, 2, with $S_1=S_2$. Let $Y_{\ge3}$ be $\widehat d$ computed from the remaining $d-2$ edges alone, minus $d-2$. Then
\begin{equation*}
Y=Y_{\ge3}+2s_1s_2+\frac{2}{k(1-k/M)}(s_1+s_2)\sum_{j\ge3}s_j\Bigl(|S_1\cap S_j|-\frac{k^2}{M}\Bigr).
\end{equation*}
The last term has variance $O(d/M)$. After division by $s(d)$, the deterministic term $2s_1s_2$ and the last term have mean squares $O(M/d^2)$ and $O(1/d)$, respectively. Both tend to zero. We apply the unconditional result to $Y_{\ge3}$, then use the total variation bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:plant} or \ref{lem:propL} to transfer the result to the failure-conditional distribution. The $d=o(M)$ and overload branches in the next subsection use this conclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:clt} (the Gaussian limit after failure) and Corollary~\ref{cor:gauss}}{Proof of the Gaussian limit after failure and the Gaussian rule}}
Assume $d/\sqrt M\to\infty$. From any subsequence, we take a further subsequence along which $d/M$ converges in $[0,\infty]$.

If the limit is positive and finite, we apply the previous subsection.

If the limit is zero, Lemma~\ref{lem:plant} shows that the TV distance between the failure-conditional distribution and the planted distribution $Q$ tends to zero for all $2\le d=o(M)$. This graph-theoretic result continues to hold after we add independent signs. Lemma~\ref{lem:plantclt} holds uniformly in deterministic signs under both the random mapping and the planted distribution. The fourth-order Lyapunov remainder of the edge-exposure martingale is $O_k(M/d^2+1/d)$. The normalized mean square of the correction from the planted duplicate edge pair is also $O_k(M/d^2+1/d)$. Both tend to zero when $d\gg\sqrt M$. We condition on the iid coding coefficients, apply these uniform results, and average over the signs to obtain the conditional Gaussian limit.

If the limit is infinite, eventually $d>M$. We can assign each edge removed during full peeling a cell whose degree is one at the time of removal. That cell is then empty and can witness no further removal, so full peeling requires $d\le M$. Thus $F$ occurs with certainty in this branch, and we apply Lemma~\ref{lem:plantclt} directly under the random mapping.

All three branches give uniform convergence of the conditional CDF to $\Phi$. A subsequence contradiction then establishes \eqref{eq:clt} for the full sequence. Corollary~\ref{cor:gauss} follows because the Gaussian quantile and the standardized chi-square quantile have the same limit and $\{U<d\}\subseteq\{\widehat d<t(d)\}$. \qed

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Monotonicity of the acceptance lower bound $t(n)$ in Definition~\ref{def:gauss}}{Monotonicity of the acceptance lower bound of the Gaussian rule}}\label{app:gauss-mono}
We have $t(n)=n+q\,s(n)$ with $q=[\chi^2_{M-1,u}-(M-1)]/\sqrt{2(M-1)}$. For $q\ge0$, $t$ is increasing. For $q<0$, differentiation for $n\ge2$ gives $\frac{d}{dn}s(n)\le\frac{\sqrt2(2n-1)}{2\sqrt{n(n-1)(M-1)}}\le\frac{3}{2\sqrt2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt{M-1}}$. A sufficient condition is therefore $|q|\sqrt{2/(M-1)}<\sqrt8/3$. The Chernoff bound for the chi-square lower tail \cite[Lemma~1]{laurent2000adaptive} gives $|q|\le\sqrt{2\log(1/u)}\le3.035$ for $u\ge0.01$. The condition holds when $M\ge64$. \qed
