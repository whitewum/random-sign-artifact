\section{Proofs of the discrete limit distribution and the Skellam rule}\label{app:inversion}
This appendix proves Theorems~\ref{thm:cross}, \ref{thm:uniform}, and \ref{thm:coverage}. Writing the coding coefficients as iid $\varepsilon_i$, by \eqref{eq:raw-expand} the identity $\widetilde d-d=\frac2k\sum_{i<j}H_{ij}\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j$ holds sample by sample. A final subsection discusses the extension that stores several copies of the sign count per cell.

\subsection{Exact moments for a specified duplicate edge pair}
Let $S_1=S_2$, with the remaining $d-2$ edges independent and uniform. When the two coding coefficients are opposite, the two edges cancel completely, leaving the remaining $d-2$ elements; when they are equal, they combine into a single edge with coefficient $\pm2$. For $d\ge2$,
\begin{equation*}
\E[\widehat d\mid S_1=S_2]=d,\qquad
\Var(\widehat d\mid S_1=S_2)=\frac{2d(d-1)}{M-1}+4-\frac4{M-1}.
\end{equation*}
The variance of the opposite component is $2(d-2)(d-3)/(M-1)$, and that of the equal component is $2(d-2)(d+5)/(M-1)$; averaging and then adding the $4$ arising from the difference $d\mp2$ between the two component means gives the result.

\subsection{From the true failure to the planted case}
Lemma~\ref{lem:plant} gives $\|\mathcal L(\mathcal G\mid F)-Q\|_{TV}\to0$ for all $2\le d=o(M)$, where $Q$ first picks a pair of edge labels uniformly and then conditions on this pair mapping identically. On $2\le d\le C\sqrt M$, both $\lambda$ and $d^2/M^k$ are $O_k(M^{1-k})$; since $b_k=\lceil3(k-2)/2\rceil$, $k-b_k-3=-\lceil k/2\rceil\le-2$, so $dM^{k-b_k-3}=O_k(d/M^2)$, and this distance is at most $O_k(d/M^2+M^{1-k})$. Adding independent signs and applying \eqref{eq:raw-expand} do not increase the TV distance. The statistic is invariant under label permutation, so we may fix the duplicate edge pair as 1, 2; their common edge and the remaining $d-2$ edges are independent and uniform. When $d=2$, the null distribution holds exactly under the planted case; empty sums below are treated as zero.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$c$}{c} bounded: three classes of events that can be ignored}
The probability that the common edge of the duplicate edge pair intersects any remaining edge is $O_k(d/M)$ by the union bound. The probability that two remaining edges share at least two cells is $O_k(M^{-2})$, summing over all pairs of remaining edges to $O_k(d^2/M^2)$. Consider the intersection graph of the remaining edges: for any three specified remaining edges, the probability that one of them intersects both of the other two is
\begin{equation*}
\Bigl(1-\binom{M-k}{k}\Big/\binom Mk\Bigr)^2=O_k(M^{-2}),
\end{equation*}
the equality following by conditioning on the common edge: the other two edges are independent, and the intersection probability does not depend on the position of the common edge. Summing over ordered triples, the probability that the intersection graph is not a matching is $O_k(d^3/M^2)$. Hence, except for the probability in \eqref{eq:tv-error}, the duplicated base edge and the ordinary edges are completely separated, and every overlap among the remaining edges is a single-cell overlap within a matching.

\subsection{Poisson approximation of the overlaps among the remaining edges}
Take the intersection indicator for each pair of remaining edges; the labels of the two edges form a dependency graph, where indicator sets with disjoint labels are independent. Each indicator has mean $p=1-\binom{M-k}k/\binom Mk=k^2/M+O_k(M^{-2})$, and each indicator has $O(d)$ neighbors; the joint probability of two indicators sharing an edge equals $p^2$ exactly by the computation of the previous subsection. The local dependence bound of Arratia, Goldstein, and Gordon \cite[Theorem~1]{arratia1989chen} (with the neighborhood containing the indicator itself, indicators outside the neighborhood depending only on the remaining independent edges, and the third term zero) gives: the TV distance is at most a universal constant times the sum of products of means within the neighborhood plus the sum of joint probabilities over distinct neighbors, both of which are $O_k((d^2+d^3)/M^2)$ in this model. Hence the total number of intersecting edge pairs among the remaining edges has TV distance $O_k((d^2+d^3)/M^2)$ from $\Pois(\binom{d-2}2p)$. Replacing the parameter by $\binom{d-2}2k^2/M=2\lambda_d$, the TV distance between two Poisson distributions is at most the difference of their parameters (writing the Poisson with the larger parameter as the Poisson with the smaller parameter plus an independent Poisson increment, the two differ only when the increment is nonzero), an additional error of $O_k(d^2/M^2)$.

\subsection{Independence of the overlap sign marks and the Skellam limit}
On the matching event, each overlap uses a pair of iid signs not shared with any other overlap, so these sign products are mutually independent and symmetric, and independent of the sign product $\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$ of the duplicate edge pair. Given the mapping, $\widetilde d$ depends only on the number of overlaps among the remaining edges and on these independent $\pm1$ marks. Formally, first keep the true number of overlaps, and regenerate that many independent marks for each mapping: on the matching event the conditional distribution of the old and new marked sums is the same; on the remaining mappings couple arbitrarily, the distribution difference being at most the probability of the exceptional event. Then apply the same marking kernel to the Poisson approximation of the count, which does not increase the TV distance. By the thinning property of the Poisson distribution \cite{kingman1993poisson}, $\Pois(2\lambda_d)$ independent $\pm1$ marks split by sign into two independent $\Pois(\lambda_d)$ counts, and the difference of the positive and negative counts is a Skellam-distributed \cite{skellam1946} $Z_d$; adding $2\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$ of the duplicate edge pair gives $J_{\lambda_d}$ in \eqref{eq:J}, that is, $\widetilde d$ is close to the null distribution $d+J_{\lambda_d}$ \eqref{eq:aux}. This step uses only the Poisson approximation of the unconditional count; the matching event is used only for the coupling error.

Adding the errors of the four steps gives $O_k(d/M+d^2/M^2+d^3/M^2+d/M^2+M^{1-k})$. Since $d\le M$ and $k\ge3$, every term except $d/M$ and $d^3/M^2$ is absorbed by these two, yielding \eqref{eq:tv-error}. When $d/\sqrt M\to c$, $\lambda_d\to k^2c^2/4$; the Poisson parameter is continuous, and $J_{\lambda_d}$ converges in total variation to $J_{k^2c^2/4}$, giving Theorem~\ref{thm:cross}(ii); since the $\pm2$ term and the Skellam term are independent, $\Var J_\lambda=4+8\lambda/k^2$, and the limiting variance is $4+2c^2$. For (iii), as $\lambda\to0$, $\Pr(Z\ne0)\le2\lambda\to0$ and $J_\lambda\Rightarrow2\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$; as $\lambda\to\infty$, $Z/\sqrt{2\lambda}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)$, while $\sqrt{8\lambda}/k=\sqrt2\,c$, and the $\pm2$ term divided by $\sqrt2\,c$ tends to zero, so $J_{k^2c^2/4}/(\sqrt2\,c)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)$. $\widehat d$ has the same weak limit, because the coefficient of the AMS term in \eqref{eq:raw-decomp} is $k/M$, while $(\sum_i\varepsilon_i)^2=O_p(d)=O_p(\sqrt M)$. \qed

\subsection{Sparse limit for fixed small \texorpdfstring{$d$}{d}}
\begin{proposition}[Limit when $d=o(\sqrt M)$]\label{prop:smalld}
Fix $k\ge3$, and let $2\le d=o(\sqrt M)$. Given failure, both $\widehat d-d$ and $\widetilde d-d$ converge weakly to the equally weighted $\pm2$. The conditional miss rate of the Gaussian rule tends to zero for bounded $d=2,3$, and to $1/2$ for fixed $d\ge4$ or $d\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cross} gives the equally weighted $\pm2$ limit of $\widetilde d$ when $d=o(\sqrt M)$; by \eqref{eq:raw-decomp}, the correction of $\widehat d$ is $O_p(d^2/M)=o_p(1)$, so it has the same weak limit. On the event that the edges other than the duplicate edge pair are pairwise disjoint and also disjoint from the duplicate edge pair, $\widetilde d$ equals $d+2\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$ exactly; the conditional probability of this event tends to one. The Gaussian rule has $t(d)=d-O(s(d))$ with $s(d)\to0$: the negative component rejects the true candidate, and the positive component accepts it. For $d=2,3$, the observation under the negative component is below $t(2)$, returning $+\infty$ and not counted as a miss; for $d\ge4$, the negative component still accepts the candidate $d-2$, causing a miss. When $d=2$, failure is exactly equivalent to a duplicate edge, and $\Pr(\widehat d=0\mid F)=\Pr(\widehat d=4\mid F)=1/2$ holds for every $M>k$; this also shows that no upper bound obtained by multiplying $\widehat d$ by a fixed constant can reduce the post-failure risk below $1/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of the uniform CDF approximation}
\begin{proof}
The branch $d/\sqrt M\to\infty$: Appendix~\ref{app:clt} gives the conditional Gaussian limit of $\widetilde d$ after standardizing by $s(d)$. The null distribution is that of $d+J_{\lambda_d}$. As $\lambda_d\to\infty$, the $Z_d$ in the Skellam term can be written as a sum of iid increments that grows with $\lambda_d$, and the CLT gives $(2/k)Z_d/\sqrt{8\lambda_d/k^2}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)$; moreover $8\lambda_d/k^2=2(d-2)(d-3)/M$ has ratio to $s(d)^2$ tending to one, and the $\pm2$ term divided by $s(d)$ tends to zero, so $J_{\lambda_d}/s(d)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)$. Both, after standardization, tend to the continuous $\Phi$; when the limit is continuous, weak convergence implies uniform convergence of the CDF (P\'olya's theorem), so the Kolmogorov distance tends to zero.

Uniformity: suppose there exist $\delta>0$ and a sequence $(M,d)$ along which the distance is at least $\delta$. Take a subsequence with $d/\sqrt M\to c\in[0,\infty]$. For $c<\infty$, this subsequence eventually falls in a range $d\le C\sqrt M$, where Theorem~\ref{thm:cross} gives TV distance tending to zero; for $c=\infty$, the previous paragraph gives the same. Both contradict $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of the conditional miss rate of the Skellam rule}
\begin{proof}
Write $G_n$ for the CDF of the null distribution at candidate $n$ and $Y$ for the observation. The rule rejects $n$ if and only if $G_n(Y)\le u$. The following is the standard argument for the conservativeness of test inversion with a discrete p-value \cite{neyman1937}. If $Y\sim G_n$, then $\Pr(G_n(Y)\le u)\le u$: when no lattice point satisfies $G_n(y)\le u$, the rejection event is empty; otherwise let $y^*$ be the largest lattice point with $G_n(y)\le u$, so the rejection event is $\{Y\le y^*\}$ and has probability $G_n(y^*)\le u$. The rejection region $\{Y\le y^*\}$ is a lower set, so the difference between the rejection probability under the true distribution and under $G_n$ is at most their Kolmogorov distance. Taking $n=d$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} the rejection probability of the true candidate is at most $u+o(1)$ uniformly over all $d\ge2$. The event $\{U<d\}$ implies that the true candidate is rejected, yielding \eqref{eq:coverage}. This proof requires neither that the acceptance set be an interval nor that the rejection probability of a candidate be monotone in $n$.
\end{proof}

Rejecting by $\le u$ using the left limit $G_n(Y-)=\Pr(Y^{\prime}<Y)$ (with $Y^{\prime}\sim G_n$) would yield a different rule: the equally weighted $\pm2$ has left limit zero at $-2$, so the minimal value point carrying probability $1/2$ would be rejected, and the conditional miss rate would tend to $1/2$ when $d\ge4$ and $d=o(\sqrt M)$. At that point $G_n$ equals $1/2>u$, so it is accepted.

When $d=3$, the observation need not lie on a value point of $G_3$, but $\widetilde d\ge1$ always. When the three coefficients have the same sign, $\sum_vC_v^2\ge3k$. Otherwise, label the edge whose sign differs from the other two as edge $3$, so the sum of squares is $3k+2(H_{12}-H_{13}-H_{23})$; among the $k$ cells of edge $3$, the number shared with edge $1$ or edge $2$ is at least $H_{13}+H_{23}-H_{12}$, so $H_{13}+H_{23}-H_{12}\le k$, and the sum of squares is at least $k$. Hence $G_3(\widetilde d)\ge G_3(1)=1/2>u$, and the true candidate is always accepted.

\subsection{Combining the two rules by the first-round observation}\label{app:switch}
The combined rule takes the upper bound of the Gaussian rule when $\widetilde d$ is not below the switch point, and the upper bound of the Skellam rule otherwise. Assume that the ratio of the switch point to $\sqrt{M}$ tends to infinity as $M\to\infty$; we show that the combined rule satisfies \eqref{eq:coverage}.

\begin{proof}
Suppose there exist $\delta>0$ and a sequence $(M,d)$ along which the combined rule has $\Pr(U<d\mid F)\ge u+\delta$. Take a subsequence with $d/\sqrt M\to c\in[0,\infty]$.

For $c<\infty$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} the distribution of $\widetilde d-d$ after failure and that of $J_{\lambda_d}$ have distance tending to zero, and $\lambda_d$ is bounded, so $\widetilde d=d+O_p(1)=O_p(\sqrt M)$, and the probability of lying above the switch point tends to zero. The combined rule and the Skellam rule differ only on this event, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} the miss probability is at most $u+o(1)$.

For $c=\infty$, a miss means that the rule selected by the observation rejected the true candidate $d$. The Skellam rule rejects $d$ if and only if $(\widetilde d-d)/s(d)$ is below the lower $u$-quantile of the standardized $G_d$; by the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}, $J_{\lambda_d}/s(d)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)$, and this quantile tends to $\Phi^{-1}(u)$. The Gaussian rule rejects $d$ if and only if $(\widehat d-d)/s(d)<(t(d)-d)/s(d)$; the right side also tends to $\Phi^{-1}(u)$, and $(\widehat d-\widetilde d)/s(d)\to0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:ams}). By Theorem~\ref{thm:clt}, the conditional distribution of the standardized statistic tends to the continuous $\mathcal N(0,1)$, so the events on which the two rules reject $d$ differ only on a boundary region of probability tending to zero. The rejection event of the combined rule is contained in the union of the two, and its probability is at most the rejection probability of the Skellam rule plus $o(1)$, namely $u+o(1)$.

Both cases contradict $\delta>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Extension to multiple sign counts}\label{app:multi}
If each cell stores $L$ copies of the count, with the copies using independent sign hashes and sharing the mapping, then given $\mathcal G$ the $\widehat d$ of the copies are mutually independent, each with mean $d$; averaging independent copies of the estimate is exactly how AMS reduces variance \cite{alon1999spacecomplexity}. From $\E[\widehat d^2\mid\mathcal G]\le3d^2$ and $e^{-x}\le1-x+x^2/2$,
\begin{equation*}
\E\Bigl[\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\widehat d}{5d}\Bigr)\Bigm|\mathcal G\Bigr]\le\frac{43}{50},
\end{equation*}
and applying the exponential Markov inequality (Chernoff bound) to $U=\frac2L\sum_\ell\widehat d^{(\ell)}$ gives $\Pr(U<d\mid\mathcal G)\le(\frac{43}{50}e^{1/10})^L$. This bound holds for every deterministic mapping, hence for every rare failure; when $L=272$, the right side is below $10^{-6}$. When only an extra scalar AMS accumulator is stored, the $L$ count sums are independent given $d$, with joint distribution a product of binomial distributions, allowing an exact discrete candidate test, and the finite-sample guarantee $\Pr(U<d\mid F)\le u$ holds directly.

Repeating counts reduces the absolute error but does not change the ratio of the conditional scale to the unconditional scale. Fix $d\ge4$ and $L$, and let $M\to\infty$: if Gaussian inversion is still applied to the average of $L$ copies using the unconditional standard deviation $s(n)/\sqrt L$, the actual conditional miss rate tends to $2^{-L}\sum_{j<L/2}\binom Lj$, namely $1/2,1/4,5/16,93/256$ for $L=1,2,4,8$. Hence increasing the number of copies cannot replace the correct post-failure distribution, which is why the main text uses a single count together with the discrete limit distribution.
