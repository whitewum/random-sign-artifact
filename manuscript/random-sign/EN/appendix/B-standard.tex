\section{Post-failure bias of the standard IBLT}\label{app:std}
We prove Proposition~\ref{prop:projection} and verify it numerically.

In the standard IBLT, the count is $C_v=\sum_{i:v\in S_i}s_i$, with the set direction as the coefficient. Expanding $\widehat d$ gives
\begin{equation}
\widehat d-d=\frac{2}{k(1-k/M)}\sum_{i<j}s_is_j\Bigl(H_{ij}-\frac{k^2}{M}\Bigr).\label{eq:std-expand}
\end{equation}
Failure changes the overlap distribution. Overlap between same-sign pairs raises $\widehat d$, whereas overlap between opposite-sign pairs lowers $\widehat d$. This yields Proposition~\ref{prop:projection}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:projection}]
By~\eqref{eq:std-expand},
\begin{equation*}
\E[\widehat d\mid F]-d=\frac{2}{k(1-k/M)}\sum_{i<j}s_is_j\,\E\Bigl[H_{ij}-\frac{k^2}{M}\Bigm|F\Bigr].
\end{equation*}
The event $F$ depends only on the unsigned mapping and is invariant under permutations of the key labels. Since the random mapping $\mathcal G$ is exchangeable in the labels, $\E[H_{ij}\mid F]$ is the same for all $i<j$ and equals $k^2/M+k(1-k/M)c_F$. The number of same-sign pairs minus the number of opposite-sign pairs is $\binom{d_+}2+\binom{d_-}2-d_+d_-=(b^2-d)/2$. Substituting gives $\E[\widehat d\mid F]-d=c_F(b^2-d)$, and dividing by $d$ yields~\eqref{eq:projection}. The proof uses only these two properties of $F$, so it holds for any event that depends only on the unsigned mapping, is invariant under label permutation, and has positive probability.
\end{proof}

We directly verify Proposition~\ref{prop:projection} using the simulations in Figure~\ref{fig:bias} of Section~\ref{sec:model} and Figure~\ref{fig:composition} of Section~\ref{sec:exp-decode}. Within each configuration, the five sign compositions share the same batch of mappings. Since $F$ depends only on the unsigned mapping, their failure samples coincide trial by trial and share the same $c_F$. The other factor on the right, $b^2/d-1$, depends only on the sign composition and equals $d-1$ for a one-sided difference. Thus the conditional bias for any sign composition equals the one-sided bias multiplied by $(b^2-d)/(d^2-d)$. Once we determine $c_F$ from a single one-sided point, we can predict the bias at every other level without fitting. Figure~\ref{fig:bias} shows this prediction against $(b/d)^2$. The one-sided point at $(b/d)^2=1$ determines the line for each configuration, shown in a different color. The remaining four points fall on that line, confirming~\eqref{eq:projection}.

The full verification covers 20 configurations with $k=3,4,5,6$ and failure probabilities ranging from 0.10 to 0.90 on the $M=16384$ grid. Each configuration has five sign composition levels, $d_-/d\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.4,0.5\}$. Figure~\ref{fig:bias} shows ten of these configurations with $k=3$ and $k=6$. The standard-encoding curves in Figure~\ref{fig:composition}(a) show four configurations with failure probability about 0.1, including $k=4$ and $k=5$. The four non-one-sided sign composition levels in each of the 20 configurations give 80 predictions in total. In units of $s(d)$, the differences between predictions and measurements are at most 0.0064 and all lie within two standard errors. The standard error includes sampling error in both the one-sided mean and the measured value.
