\begin{abstract}
In a two-round set reconciliation protocol based on an Invertible Bloom Lookup Table (IBLT), the first round sends a fixed-capacity table. If an oversized difference causes decoding failure, the receiver must estimate a safe upper bound on the unknown difference size $d$ from the sum of squared counts in the difference table to size the second-round table adaptively. The standard IBLT uses each element's set direction as its accumulation sign. This couples the hypergraph-overlap bias induced by failure selection with the sign composition of the two-sided difference. A one-sided difference amplifies the estimation bias through contributions of the same sign, forcing existing schemes to rely on extensive offline simulation calibration over a grid of configurations.

We propose the \emph{random-sign IBLT}. A pseudorandom sign independent of the position mapping decouples failure selection from sign composition while preserving the number and width of cell fields and the peeling recovery capability. We prove that the estimator is exactly unbiased on every fixed failed hypergraph. For large differences, we prove a Gaussian limit for the estimation error conditioned on failure and derive a closed-form Gaussian upper bound. For small differences, we characterize a discrete limit distribution dominated by duplicate edge pairs and construct a Skellam upper-bound rule that strictly controls the conditional miss rate for all $d\ge2$ without offline calibration. Five hundred million paired trials and end-to-end experiments with the real protocol spanning several orders of magnitude in load show that the random-sign IBLT eliminates direction sensitivity and strictly meets the prescribed risk. Its total two-round communication cost approaches the ideal lower bound for a known difference size.
\end{abstract}
