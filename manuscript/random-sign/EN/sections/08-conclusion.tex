\section{Discussion and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
The random-sign IBLT uses a single table for both decoding and difference-size estimation, preserving the cells and decoding capability. The mapping alone determines decoding success, while the signs supply the estimate's randomness. This separation makes the estimate exactly unbiased for every fixed mapping and identically distributed across sign compositions. It also lets us fully characterize the post-failure distribution: Gaussian when $d\gg\sqrt M$, and discrete with a $\pm2$ term plus a Skellam term when $c=d/\sqrt M$ is bounded. One family of distributions describes all scales. Using this family as the null distribution, the Skellam rule keeps the conditional miss rate asymptotically at most the target risk, uniformly over all $d\ge2$.

For deployment, we recommend combining the two rules based on the first-round observation. This retains the uniform guarantee for all $d\ge2$ and matches the Gaussian rule's runtime in the overload region. Section~\ref{sec:exp-deploy} gives the guarantees and costs of each choice. The Skellam rule meets the target on all experimental configurations with $M\ge256$, and we recommend $M\ge1024$ for deployment (Section~\ref{sec:exp-cover}). Shortening the checksum to 32 bits yields narrower cells while preserving the sizing risk. The verification error probability remains at most $10^{-4}$ at $M_1=16384$ and load at most 3 (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). Storing counts for several independent signs per cell reduces absolute error while preserving the post-failure conditional scale. Inversion still uses the post-failure distribution (Appendix~\ref{app:multi}). Random-sign encoding preserves cell space overhead, and the additional decoding time is less than 1\% of total protocol time (Section~\ref{sec:exp-cost}).

Three questions remain for future work. First, rigorous finite-parameter tail upper bounds could reject more candidates before constructing the Poisson tables, reducing the Skellam rule's computational cost. Second, making the error constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:cross} and Theorem~\ref{thm:clt} computable would give a finite risk bound for a specified $M$. Third, we seek to extend the method to multiset differences whose weights can exceed one. In this setting, the random-sign count gives an unbiased estimate of the difference vector's $F_2$. Decoding and post-failure analysis for this extension remain to be developed.
