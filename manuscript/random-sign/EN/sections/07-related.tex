\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{IBLT and set reconciliation.} IBLT\cite{gm} and the invertible Bloom filter\cite{eppstein2011whatsdifference} list the set difference by peeling, and their success or failure is determined by the 2-core of a random hypergraph\cite{molloy,dw,dm}; at low load, failure is dominated by duplicate edge pairs\cite{gm,yw}, and the probability of a linear core at subcritical load is exponentially small\cite{kim2008poisson,dn08}. Subsequent work improves the hypergraph structure\cite{lazaro2021irregular}, gives rateless codes\cite{yang2024rateless,lazaro2023ratecompatible}, or predicts and breaks the 2-core after a decoding failure\cite{gadurek2026breaking}. Belazzougui, Kucherov, and Walzer\cite{belazzougui2024better} use markers over a finite field to distinguish the direction of the difference and reduce the failure probability to exponentially small. These works focus on how to reduce failure or continue decoding from failure; this paper focuses on how to read the same table after failure to decide the next-round capacity.

\paragraph{Estimating the difference size.} The common way to estimate $d$ is to send a separate estimator: the strata estimator\cite{eppstein2011whatsdifference}, min-wise hashing\cite{broder2000minwise}, the odd sketch\cite{mitzenmacher2014odd}, and GXBits\cite{jia2024gxbits}. PBS\cite{gong2021pbs} uses 128 independent ToW sketches to first estimate $d$ and then sets the capacity to a fixed multiple of the estimate, with the estimator and the reconciliation structure transmitted and accounted separately. The estimate in this paper is read on the same set of counts used for decoding, without sending a separate estimator; the cost is that the estimate is needed only when decoding fails, and failure selects the mapping, which is exactly the object of the analysis in this paper.

\paragraph{Random-sign sketches and second-moment estimation.} AMS\cite{alon1999spacecomplexity} estimates $F_2$ by a sum of random signs; CountSketch\cite{charikar2004countsketch} uses random-sign counters to support point queries; Thorup and Zhang\cite{thorup2004tabulation} analyze its second-moment extension and give a centered second-moment estimate for a single counter array, and Fast-AGMS\cite{cormode2005fastagms} uses the same structure for distributed queries. $\widehat d$ has the same algebraic form as these estimators, and its conditional unbiasedness given the mapping also has the structure of the Hutchinson trace estimator\cite{hutchinson1990trace}. These sketches carry no decodable keySum and do not involve the conditional distribution of a decoding failure. Fischer and Nakos\cite{fischer2025sparse} combine hypergraph peeling with a random-sign CountSketch in a single measurement, but the tail norm is provided by an independent short sketch and treated as known in the analysis. This paper writes the shared random signs into the count, keySum, and checksum of the IBLT, so that the same table can peel and, after failure, give an estimate independent of the direction of the difference, together with a post-failure upper bound valid for all loads.

\paragraph{Reading the same table after failure.} The work \cite{wu2026selfsizing} reads the count energy after a first-round failure to set the second-round capacity, treating iid signs as a statistical model of the sign composition in the analysis and obtaining a conditional mean equal to $d$; its upper-bound rule uses a chi-square approximation under deep overload and relies on per-configuration simulation calibration under other loads. This paper writes the random signs into the three fields of the IBLT as the encoding, making these properties hold exactly for every fixed mapping, and gives a post-failure distribution and upper bound valid for all loads.

\paragraph{Conditional limits and discrete approximations.} The limit distribution when $c$ (\eqref{eq:c}) is bounded uses the Chen--Stein method\cite{arratia1989chen} to handle the Poisson approximation of sparse overlap, and the Skellam distribution\cite{skellam1946} describes the difference of two kinds of counts. At low load, the failure-conditional distribution is replaced by the planted distribution, which is the size-biased distribution of the duplicate edge pair count\cite{arratia2019sizebias}. When $d\gg\sqrt M$, the random-sign quadratic form given the mapping is handled by the cumulant method on the dependency graph\cite{janson1988cumulant}, needing only control of the concentration\cite{warnke2016typical,combes2015mcdiarmid} and the degree of the overlap graph; the Gaussian limit for deterministic coefficients is proved by an edge-exposure martingale CLT\cite{hall1980martingale}, and a general quadratic form CLT is in\cite{dejong}. The upper-bound rule uses the classical test inversion\cite{neyman1937}.
